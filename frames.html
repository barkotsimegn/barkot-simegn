Fiker Frames

Overview

Love is in the air. I initially designed a custom picture frame for my significant other and some friends told me they wanted one. This inspired me to redesign what I made and change it from a cute little gift, to something people would pay me money for. In the end I sold 9 frames and got positive reception inspiring me to potentially use this to fundraise for a club I am in. Overall, this success taught me how to design for people and develop skills such as field research, taking suggestions, iterating, and the importance of presentation.

Models Over the Years

I was inspired by a friend to undertake this project. From our conversation we realized that people spend upwards of hundreds of dollars on presents for Valentine's Day, often times overspending on specific items like picture frames. So I started researching, looking at what frames exist and what couples typically buy. From this I saw a market for custom engraved frames for about 20-30 dollars per frame with no picture included. I figured that I could undercut that given the machinery I have access to. After 3 days of initial ideation and prototyping, I had a product that people could give me feedback on. Initially, the low fidelity mockups I had people review were not recieved positively. This taught me the importance of showing people a more finalized product and filling in as many details as possible for my audience. Eventually I ended with a final product people enjoyed and went to market. It was a moderate success, and made about 50 dollars in profit. Overall, the goal was not to make money, but to explore the product life cycle and consumer design.

Learning

In completing this project, I learned how hard and uncomfortable it is to be an entrepreneur. I felt like a pauper when selling my product and it was very uncomfortable. From that discomfort came growth, my sales pitch became shorter and eventually people listened as Valentine's Day came closer and closer. I learned to design for the people, and sell to the masses and what does and doesn’t work.
