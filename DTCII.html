Design Thinking and Communication II

Overview

This project taught me compassionate design and the emphasis on a design purpose. A solution with no problem is a problem, so it’s important to address the key issues and solve those. For this project, I worked in a team of 4 people to research, design, and prototype a solution for a future Northwestern student that needs a modified bike due to a pre-existing condition.

Models Over the Years

Our team worked closely with the Shirley Ryan Ability Lab in Chicago to gather data and consult our client to create a design that was functional and they apporved of. This was important because beyond the technical solution needed, a “normal looking” design was desired from our client. This was to say, our client felt ostracized due to having a disability and didn’t want to draw attention to it. Rather, they wanted to have something that looked and felt like a normal bike, which is also why commercial mobility bikes didn’t work for them. So we had our design purpose and our design mission, create an accessory to any commercially avalible bike, so that she can ride it comfortably and safely. So we built initial mockups, tested them and eventually we designed a mockup in Solidworks that we presented as our final design. We met the design challenge infront of us and most importantly, did so while keeping our clients needs at the forefront of our minds.

Learning

I learned how to tackle issues holistically. Design challenges are multifaceted and most of my education to this point was on how to technically solve problems. That is, use numbers and hard data to reach conclusions. However, so much gets lost when you boil issues down to statistics, and this project helped contextualize the importance of the human element in design.
