Overview

As an aspiring engineer, I want to push my abilities to their limits whenever I can so I can grow and learn to become a better engineer. Wall-e was the start of that learning process for me. Most of what I know in Solidworks came from this independent project and while I have refined my skills since, the journey of designing and bringing to life a piece with over 70 components and over 100 solid bodies joined in an assembly was long and fruitful.

Models Over the Years

My favorite movie growing up was the Incredibles but a close second was Wall-e. As I was growing up and developing my skills. I undertook a challenge, creating Wall-e entirely in Solidworks to ultimately 3D print and bring to life. This process started from two pictures, no orthographic drawings, no dimensions given, just two pictures and my imagination. This forced me to create and fill in the details, and when pieces didn’t fit together how I imagined, it forced me to come up with new solutions and add my own spin to this beloved character. Bringing Wall-e to life taught me even more lessons. Unfortunately, after printing my final mockup the neck snapped. This taught me the importance of testing components virtually before committing resources to it. Lastly, Wall-e is a robot and I learned how to program a basic remote control set up with VEX motors to try and drive him around. Again, having not done a stress analysis, it broke almost immediately, reinforcing the importance of testing virtually before making something real.

Learning

This project taught me Solidworks, and the skills I learned from this project are the skills that I used to get my Certified Solidworks Associate and Professional Certificates. Additionally, this project taught me the limits of polymer based additive manufacturing and gave me more realistic expectations moving forward.
