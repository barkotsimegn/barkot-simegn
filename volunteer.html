Volunteering

Overview

I volunteer for various STEM outreach programs for underprivlaged you in Evanston, in colaboration with the Evanston Public Library, Evanston Y.O.U. and Northwestern's chapter of the National Society of Black Engineers. In this collaboration, we are given students to mentor and teach basic engineering skills to complete some kind of STEM challenge, all while inspiring them to keep learning about engineering and maybe even become one.

Day to Day

Being a volunteer and working with youth, a balence needs to be struck. Teaching them, while allowing them to be curious and explore on their own. A really big thing kids struggle with from my observations is exploring in novel spaces. For many of them, if it's something they love like football or racing, they can tell you all about it, but the moment it's a computer or a sketch, they freeze up and expect me to do it for them. Giving them the confidence to try things out, giving them ideas on where to start, and answering their questions is what I did every Saturday I volunteered. However, I had to make sure in giving them guidence I wasn't giving them answers. It's this balence that makes the job fun and being able to see kid's faces when their initial sketches become a real product always made me happy.

Learning

I learned how to communicate ideas with people that might not have the same background or education as me. While teaching kids is an extreme, it's important to be able to communicate ideas to any audience, and teaching kids showed me ways to break down ideas and make them relatable to them.
